# Teswa Phase 00 — Meaning Extraction Gate

**Date:** 2026-09-17  
**Branch:** `audit/native-product-reality-20260917`  
**Status:** EXTRACTION COMPLETE — SMALL MEANING SYSTEM SURVIVED

## Purpose

Phase 00 has now accumulated enough evidence.

We have:
- current Native/Oracle product contracts,
- explicit item / offer / deal / review state transitions,
- Dolab's private-workspace behavior,
- local Egyptian second-hand / inspection / meetup evidence,
- broader research on ownership, information asymmetry, reputation and proposals.

The job now is **subtraction**.

This document deliberately kills attractive ideas that are too broad, too cliché, too narrow, or too weak to organize the product.

No visual references are used here.

---

# 1. Gate criteria

A candidate survives only if it passes all of these tests:

1. **Product relevance** — it is visible in real Teswa behavior/contracts.
2. **Source depth** — it is supported by more than a clever phrase.
3. **Generative power** — it can coherently affect several product layers.
4. **Anti-cliché strength** — it does not collapse into generic marketplace language.
5. **Usability compatibility** — it can coexist with precise transactional state.
6. **Native/device opportunity** — it can become interaction/system behavior, not only brand copy.

Labels:
- `LOCK` — survives as a product-system principle.
- `SUPPORT` — important but subordinate to a stronger principle.
- `DEMOTE` — useful as a local mechanic or source metaphor, not a system principle.
- `KILL` — do not use to author the product.

---

# 2. Candidate: DORMANT VALUE

**Decision:** `KILL AS THESIS`

Why it was attractive:
- an unused possession can produce value for another person,
- it explains why old / idle objects re-enter circulation.

Why it dies:
- “unlock dormant value” is generic startup / marketplace language,
- it pushes the product toward an abstract economic story,
- it does not explain Dolab's private state, trust, inspection, mutual completion or person-to-person context,
- it makes “value” sound more objective than Teswa's own proposal mechanics imply.

What survives from it:

> An owned object can become relevant again through another person.

That idea is absorbed into a stronger principle below.

---

# 3. Candidate: USEFULNESS MOVES

**Decision:** `DEMOTE`

Strength:
- locally grounded distinction: no longer useful to me does not mean worthless,
- human and less financial than “dormant value”.

Why it does not become the product thesis:
- usefulness alone cannot explain expressive/unique/identity-driven objects,
- it does not explain why proposals, trust and mutual confirmation exist,
- it can become sentimental marketing language too easily.

Use later for:
- editorial/copy exploration,
- certain empty states or stories,
- source metaphor.

Do not build the architecture around it.

---

# 4. Candidate: POSSESSION → POSSIBILITY

**Decision:** `LOCK — PRIMARY MEANING PRINCIPLE`

Why it survives:

## Product evidence

Teswa already has a literal private-to-public path:

```text
Dolab draft / private material
→ ready
→ publish source
→ marketplace item
→ active availability
→ another person discovers it
→ an offer becomes possible
```

Add Item does more than describe an object. It carries owner openness through `DesireMode` and desire text.

Offer creation then binds:

```text
what you have ↔ what I have
```

The object does not begin as “inventory for sale.” It begins as something a person already owns.

## Source depth

V1:
- ownership changes valuation,
- possessions can participate in identity,
- used goods create information asymmetry.

V2:
- unused to me ≠ worthless,
- physical exchange converts digital possibility into real handoff,
- local second-hand behavior includes practical, economic and social motives.

## Generative power

This principle can organize:
- Dolab,
- Add Item,
- publishing,
- item representation,
- discovery,
- offers,
- transition states,
- empty states,
- motion/gesture metaphors,
- ownership/history treatment.

## Anti-cliché test

It is not “shop smarter,” “sell faster,” “circular marketplace,” or “community commerce.”

It describes Teswa's actual threshold:

> **a private possession becomes a shared possibility only when a person exposes it and another person can answer it.**

## Locked principle

> **TESWA TURNS POSSESSIONS INTO POSSIBILITIES BETWEEN PEOPLE.**

This is product-system language, not final marketing copy.

# 5. Candidate: PROPOSAL IS THE ATOMIC UNIT

**Decision:** `SUPPORT — COMMITMENT PRINCIPLE`

Why it survives partially:
- offer is a real typed object,
- it is one requested item against one owned active item,
- it has explicit response states,
- acceptance creates a new `dealId`.

Why it is not primary:
- Teswa begins before proposals: possession, preparation, publication, discovery and trust,
- Teswa continues after them: coordination, physical completion, review.

Corrected principle:

> **THE PROPOSAL IS THE FIRST EXPLICIT COMMITMENT THRESHOLD.**

That gives Offers a special role without pretending the whole product starts there.

Implication for future design:
- proposal should not feel like ordinary chat text,
- its state/history should remain legible,
- “thinking”, rejection and acceptance should feel meaningfully different.

# 6. Candidate: CERTAINTY IS EARNED IN LAYERS

**Decision:** `LOCK — TRUST PRINCIPLE`

Why it survives:

Teswa can never know an object or stranger through one field.

Current evidence accumulates through:

```text
owner claim
+ media
+ condition / notes
+ item story
+ profile history
+ response behavior
+ conversation
+ location / meetup feasibility
+ physical inspection
+ mutual completion
+ post-deal review
```

Local Egyptian source evidence strengthens inspection and safe meetup as real thresholds rather than generic safety copy.

Reviews then feed outcome evidence back into future decisions.

Locked principle:

> **CERTAINTY IS NOT SHOWN ONCE. IT IS EARNED IN LAYERS.**

Design consequence later:
- never compress all trust into one score/badge,
- reveal the evidence most relevant to the current threshold,
- distinguish claims from outcome-backed evidence.

This principle spans item truth, profile trust, offer confidence, deal coordination and review.

---

# 7. Candidate: AGREEMENT IS NOT COMPLETION

**Decision:** `LOCK — REAL-WORLD THRESHOLD PRINCIPLE`

Why it survives:
- offer acceptance creates a deal, not a completed exchange,
- deal state moves into coordination,
- both participants must separately confirm completion,
- only then does review become available,
- local source evidence shows inspection / meetup / handoff are real physical stages.

Locked principle:

> **DIGITAL AGREEMENT CREATES A COMMITMENT TO MEET REALITY; IT DOES NOT CLAIM REALITY ALREADY HAPPENED.**

This is important because Teswa crosses the phone boundary.

Future design must not make accepted offers visually indistinguishable from completed swaps.

---

# 8. Candidate: FAIRNESS IS RELATIONAL

**Decision:** `SUPPORT / RESEARCH`

Why it matters:
- barter value can differ by need, condition and personal preference,
- local swap behavior shows participants may discuss gaps or compensation,
- Teswa's own one-item-for-one-item proposal already implies subjective comparison.

Why it is not locked as a product principle:
- current Native contract does not support multi-item bundles or cash compensation,
- “fairness” is difficult for the system to infer without becoming paternalistic or falsely objective,
- there is not yet enough Teswa user evidence to know how users reason about value gaps.

Keep as an Offers research question:

> What evidence helps two people judge an exchange for themselves without Teswa pretending to calculate fairness for them?

# 9. Candidate: PRIVATE WORLD ↔ PUBLIC POSSIBILITY

**Decision:** `LOCK — ARCHITECTURE PRINCIPLE`

This emerged from Dolab and became much stronger after contract archaeology.

Dolab can hold:
- unpublished items,
- drafts / ready state,
- notes,
- media,
- exchange intent,
- material saved from direct conversations,
- voice material,
- a link to the published marketplace object when it crosses outward.

Dolab content can also be shared back into conversation.

Therefore:

```text
PRIVATE PERSONAL WORLD
    ↕
prepare / remember / collect / capture
    ↕
PUBLIC EXCHANGE POSSIBILITY
```

Locked principle:

> **TESWA MUST RESPECT THE BOUNDARY BETWEEN WHAT IS MINE PRIVATELY AND WHAT I CHOOSE TO PUT INTO PLAY.**

This is stronger than “Dolab is a main tab.”

It means future architecture must preserve a meaningful private/public threshold whether Dolab remains a named destination or is distributed through the system.

---

# 10. Candidate: COMMUNITY

**Decision:** `KILL AS ORGANIZING PRINCIPLE`

Why:
- Stories, People, follow and messages can create social energy,
- but “community” explains too little about why exchange works,
- it would let generic engagement features justify themselves without strengthening possession → possibility → commitment → outcome.

Community may emerge from repeated trustworthy exchange.

It is not permission to add social surfaces.

---

# 11. Candidate: TREASURE HUNT / DISCOVERY MAGIC

**Decision:** `KILL AS THESIS; KEEP AS DISCOVERY QUALITY`

Unexpected discovery can be joyful and useful.

But:
- some users will arrive with exact intent,
- trust and feasibility matter more than surprise at later thresholds,
- it does not explain offers/deals/Dolab.

Keep “surprise” as one possible discovery mode — already reflected in `DesireMode.SURPRISE` — not as the whole product identity.

---

# 12. Candidate: SUSTAINABILITY / CIRCULARITY

**Decision:** `KILL AS PRIMARY USER PROMISE`

Reuse can produce environmental benefit.

But current local/source evidence does not justify assuming sustainability is the primary motivation for Teswa's intended users.

It can remain:
- an outcome,
- a secondary narrative,
- a measurement possibility later.

Do not make it the organizing thesis.

# 13. Surviving Meaning System

After subtraction, only four principles are strong enough to organize the product.

## 1 — POSSESSION → POSSIBILITY

**Primary meaning.**

A private possession becomes a possibility between people.

## 2 — PRIVATE ↔ PUBLIC THRESHOLD

**Architecture meaning.**

What I own / save / prepare privately is not automatically public. Teswa must make crossing that boundary intentional and legible.

## 3 — CERTAINTY IS EARNED IN LAYERS

**Trust meaning.**

Claims, evidence, people, inspection and outcomes accumulate confidence over time.

## 4 — AGREEMENT ≠ COMPLETION

**Real-world meaning.**

The product must distinguish digital proposal, accepted commitment, physical coordination and actual completed exchange.

Supporting mechanic:

## PROPOSAL = FIRST EXPLICIT COMMITMENT THRESHOLD

Offers are not generic messages. They are the moment possibility becomes an explicit proposition.

---

# 14. The meaning system as one continuous product loop

```text
MY PRIVATE WORLD
    ↓ intentional release / publication
A POSSIBILITY APPEARS
    ↓ discovery + evidence
SOMEONE SEES THEMSELVES IN IT
    ↓ proposal
TWO PRIVATE WORLDS TOUCH
    ↓ response / trust / clarification
A SHARED COMMITMENT EXISTS
    ↓ coordination / inspection / meetup
REALITY CONFIRMS OR BREAKS THE AGREEMENT
    ↓ bilateral completion
THE OUTCOME BECOMES EVIDENCE
    ↓
FUTURE POSSIBILITY BECOMES EASIER TO JUDGE
```

This loop is authored from Teswa's contracts + source world.

It is not copied from another marketplace.

---

# 15. What this means for the next gate

The **Authored Product Thesis gate is now OPEN**.

The thesis must:
- center possession → possibility,
- preserve the private/public boundary,
- make trust evidence progressive rather than decorative,
- respect real-world exchange as distinct from digital agreement,
- give Offers an explicit commitment role,
- explain how Dolab, discovery, item truth, offers, deals and reviews coexist without becoming separate apps.

The thesis must NOT:
- say “marketplace” and stop,
- make commerce grids the main metaphor,
- become sustainability marketing,
- turn Teswa into generic social media,
- flatten all messaging contexts,
- imply a transaction is complete before physical reality confirms it.
