# 1. “USED” IS NOT ONE SOCIAL MEANING IN EGYPT

Recent Egyptian reporting and research show a second-hand field in motion rather than one fixed attitude.

A 2023 report on Wekalet El Balah described increased interest in used clothing even among shoppers who previously might have rejected it as a lower-status option.

A 2025 Egypt-focused study on second-hand fashion likewise describes a market where acceptance is growing while concerns around quality, hygiene, social perception and access remain meaningful.

Source relationship:

```text
used object
↔ bargain
↔ stigma
↔ trend / uniqueness
↔ practical value
```

Why this matters for Teswa:

The product should not visually or verbally assume that “used” has one emotional register.

A person may feel:
- proud of finding value
- embarrassed by second-hand status
- excited by uniqueness
- purely practical
- concerned about condition
- indifferent to the previous owner

Product question:

> Can Teswa make the object's **next usefulness** more salient than a generic “used goods” label without hiding condition truth?

Guardrail:

Do not romanticize second-hand use or pretend stigma has disappeared.

Sources:
- The National, “Cairo's thriving used-clothes market is a sign of the times,” 2023.
- Mobarak et al., “Second glance: exploring consumer shifts to thrift shopping and perception of second-hand fashion,” *Future Business Journal* 11:88, 2025, DOI `10.1186/s43093-025-00481-2`.

# 2. ECONOMIC VALUE IS REAL, BUT IT IS NOT THE ONLY VALUE

A recent August 2026 Egyptian report on used-goods markets describes households turning old goods into assets that can be sold again while other people meet needs at lower cost. The report observed large price differences between new and used goods depending on category, condition, age and repair needs.

Ipsos Egypt's September 2026 shopper report also describes price, quality and trust as major purchase drivers in the broader Egyptian shopping context.

Source relationship:

```text
unused / old possession
→ recoverable value
→ lower-cost access for someone else
```

Teswa relevance:

The economic story is credible and current.

But Teswa should not collapse into:

> “people swap because they cannot afford new things.”

That would erase:
- uniqueness
- curiosity
- decluttering
- replacement
- usefulness
- personal fit
- emotional release
- social connection
- discovery

Useful tension:

**save money ↔ find value that money alone does not describe**

Sources:
- Masrawy, “من بيت لبيت.. كيف يعيد سوق الجمعة تشغيل السلع المستعملة؟”, 13 Aug 2026.
- Ipsos Egypt, “Spotlight*Egypt — Shopper Behaviour and Attitudes,” 14 Sep 2026.

# 3. “KARAKIB” IS A PRODUCT-RELEVANT TRANSITION, NOT A BRAND WORD YET

Egyptian coverage of early barter projects repeatedly uses the everyday frame of things sitting around the home without enough current use — “كراكيب”, unused children's goods, old electronics, things one person no longer needs.

A 2021 report on an Egyptian barter project described mothers exchanging baby/child items that became useless quickly as children grew, and users exchanging things that had stopped serving them for things they currently needed.

Source relationship:

```text
still usable
+ no longer useful to me
≠ worthless
```

This distinction is extremely relevant to Teswa.

The important source concept is not the colloquial word itself. It is the change of status:

> **an object can lose relevance to one owner without losing usefulness.**

That creates a stronger meaning candidate than generic “recycling.”

Guardrails:
- do not literally brand people's possessions as junk
- do not imply owners failed by keeping unused things
- do not make every object sentimental

Sources:
- Masrawy, “استفادة بدل الكراكيب.. سيدات يطلقن مشروع مقايضة لتقليل الاستهلاك,” 2021.
- Cairo24 / Al-Ain coverage of early Egyptian barter-app activity, 2021.

# 4. INSPECTION IS PART OF TRUST, NOT AN EDGE CASE

Egypt's Consumer Protection Agency advises consumers to inspect goods carefully and not rely only on verbal assurances, especially for durable goods.

Current Egyptian peer-to-peer marketplace safety guidance likewise emphasizes:
- disclosure of defects
- physical inspection before purchase
- safe public meeting locations

Source relationship:

```text
claim
→ evidence
→ inspection
→ confidence
```

This reinforces Source Atlas v1's information-asymmetry work with local practical behavior.

Teswa implication at source level:

> Item truth may remain incomplete until the other person has had a chance to inspect, question, compare and physically encounter the object.

The product therefore should not promise certainty it cannot create.

Trust design later may need to distinguish:
- owner claim
- media evidence
- prior reputation
- platform-verified evidence, if any exists
- buyer inspection
- post-exchange outcome

Sources:
- Egypt Consumer Protection Agency consumer duties / product inspection guidance.
- Dubizzle Egypt, “How to sell and buy safely?”

# 5. MEETUP IS PART OF THE TRANSACTION MODEL

In peer-to-peer physical goods exchange, a digital agreement is not the final event.

Egyptian marketplace guidance and current swap services explicitly treat the physical meeting as a meaningful safety and completion stage.

Observed local behavior:
- choose a public place
- inspect the item
- coordinate time/location
- physically hand objects over

Source relationship:

```text
digital agreement
≠ completed exchange
```

Instead:

```text
digital agreement
→ coordination
→ physical encounter
→ inspection
→ handoff
→ actual outcome
```

This strongly matches Teswa's current bilateral completion contract.

Teswa's mutual completion confirmation is therefore not only backend ceremony. It maps onto a real-world threshold that the product cannot observe directly.

Source tension:

**agreed online ↔ actually happened**

Sources:
- Dubizzle Egypt safe-buy/sell guidance.
- Swap Egypt FAQ, which recommends official/public meeting spots.

# 6. DISTANCE IS NOT A DISCOVERY DECORATION

Current Egyptian swap products emphasize local/nearby discovery and meeting points because physical exchange has travel cost.

This gives location a more precise product meaning:

```text
attractive item
× distance
× coordination effort
× meetup confidence
= practical opportunity
```

Teswa relevance:

Location should not automatically become a map-heavy identity.

Its deeper job may be to answer:

> Is this exchange realistically worth pursuing?

That can influence:
- discovery ranking
- offer confidence
- meetup planning
- abandonment risk

The final UI form remains open.

Sources:
- Swap Egypt current FAQ / product description.
- Qayid current Egypt Play listing emphasizes nearby/local exchange.
- Dubizzle Egypt safety guidance around known public meeting points.

# 7. FAIRNESS CAN REQUIRE MORE THAN EQUAL STICKER VALUE

Current Egyptian swap behavior provides useful evidence that people do not always treat barter as strict one-object-for-one-object equality.

Swap Egypt's current FAQ explicitly describes cases where participants may discuss value differences and reach mutual agreement, including adding items or monetary compensation.

Important distinction:

This is **local market behavior evidence**, not current Teswa functionality.

Teswa's verified native offer contract today is one requested item against one offered active item.

Source relationship:

```text
item A ≠ item B by obvious market price
but exchange may still feel fair
```

Fairness can depend on:
- personal need
- urgency
- condition
- scarcity
- category
- effort
- sentimental value
- market value
- extra compensation

Source question:

> Does Teswa want to formalize “fairness”, expose evidence around it, or deliberately leave it human and negotiable?

Do not answer yet.

Source:
- Swap Egypt FAQ, current 2026 behavior description.

# 8. MULTI-ITEM EXCHANGE EXISTS LOCALLY, BUT TESWA DOES NOT SUPPORT IT TODAY

Swap Egypt currently describes multi-item exchange as a supported behavior.

That proves something about the surrounding exchange world:

> People may naturally think in bundles when trying to close a value gap.

But this must **not** leak into Teswa's redesign as assumed functionality.

Current Teswa contract remains:

```text
one requested item ↔ one offered item
```

This is exactly why source research and product-contract research must remain separate.

Source evidence can expand the question space without rewriting current truth.

Future research question:

> Is one-to-one exchange a deliberate Teswa constraint that improves clarity, or a temporary contract limitation that blocks natural behavior?

# 9. PRODUCT RISK CHANGES BY CATEGORY

Recent second-hand research across fashion, furniture and electronics found that motivations and barriers vary materially by product category.

Examples from the study:
- hygiene can matter more for some categories
- electronics can trigger stronger concern around obsolescence/warranty
- furniture can be perceived differently from fashion

This supports a source principle:

```text
“condition” is not one universal concept
```

Teswa relevance:

A phone, chair, shirt and camera do not need the same evidence.

A future item-truth system may need category-aware proof rather than one generic condition badge.

This is source inspiration only; Teswa's exact category evidence model is not designed yet.

Source:
- “Walk a mile in someone's sweaty second-hand shoes: Differences in motivations and barriers for second-hand products,” *Resources, Conservation and Recycling* 219, 2025, 108307.

# 10. EGYPTIAN GEN-Z SECOND-HAND INTEREST STILL HAS FRICTION

A 2025 Egypt-focused qualitative study of sustainable fashion / second-hand adoption reported growing interest among Gen Z alongside barriers such as:

- quality concerns
- convenience/access friction
- social attitudes
- platform trust

The study also notes that traditional sharing/borrowing practices can exist for cultural/economic reasons without participants framing them as “sustainability.”

This is useful because it warns against importing a Western circular-economy narrative as the primary emotional story.

Teswa source implication:

> Reuse can be meaningful without users needing to identify as environmental consumers.

Sustainability may be a consequence, not the first reason to act.

Source:
- “The Motivation of Generation Z to Adopt Sustainable Fashion Practices, Including the Purchase of Secondhand Items,” Egypt-focused qualitative research, 2025.

Ipsos Egypt's 2026 shopper report says trust, reputation, recommendations and reviews continue to influence choice in the broader Egyptian shopping environment.

# 12. PHYSICAL EXCHANGE HAS AN EVIDENCE GAP THE APP CANNOT FULLY CLOSE

Put the local sources together:

Before meeting, the user can know:
- photos/video
- description
- condition claim
- owner intent
- location
- reputation
- messages

At meeting, they can know more:
- actual condition
- physical size/feel
- device operation
- whether the other person appears as expected
- whether the handoff actually happens

After exchange, they can know:
- whether the item continued to match expectations
- whether the other person behaved well

Therefore:

```text
product truth develops over time
```

This strengthens — but does not yet lock — the v1 candidate phrase:

> **truth accumulates**

A better source framing may be:

> **certainty is earned in layers.**

Still a candidate, not thesis.

# 14. WHAT LOCAL EVIDENCE DOES NOT AUTHORIZE

## DO NOT conclude “Egyptians love bargaining”

That would be a stereotype without sufficient evidence.

## DO NOT make poverty the product identity

Economic pressure is real, but the product must support broader value motivations.

## DO NOT copy local marketplace UI

Behavioral evidence is allowed now. Visual reference decomposition remains closed.

## DO NOT assume cash must enter Teswa offers

Local swap services may support compensation. Teswa's current contract does not.

## DO NOT assume multi-item offers must be added

The surrounding market shows the behavior exists. Product research must decide whether complexity is worth it.

## DO NOT turn safety into fear-heavy UX

Inspection and public meetup are real needs; the product should increase clarity and agency, not create paranoia.

## DO NOT treat sustainability as the universal reason

Local evidence suggests practical/economic/social reasons can dominate.

# 15. MEANING CANDIDATES EMERGING FROM V1 + V2

These are candidates only.

## CANDIDATE A — USEFULNESS MOVES

An object's usefulness can leave one person before the object itself leaves them.

```text
not useful to me
→ potentially useful to you
```

## CANDIDATE B — POSSIBILITY IS CREATED, NOT FOUND

A possession is private/inert until someone exposes it, declares openness and another person proposes something.

```text
possession
→ openness
→ proposal
→ possibility
```

## CANDIDATE C — CERTAINTY IS EARNED IN LAYERS

Object truth and person trust accumulate through evidence, conversation, inspection, completion and review.

## CANDIDATE D — AGREEMENT IS NOT COMPLETION

The digital deal is a commitment to coordinate. The physical exchange is a separate threshold.

## CANDIDATE E — FAIRNESS IS RELATIONAL

The same two objects can feel fair or unfair depending on people, need, condition and context.

## CANDIDATE F — MY PRIVATE WORLD CAN BECOME PUBLIC VALUE

Dolab + publication + offer contracts make the boundary between private possession and public exchange unusually important.

# 16. V2 REDUCTION — WHAT IS NOW STRONGER / WEAKER

## Stronger after local research

- possession → possibility
- inspection/evidence as part of trust
- physical handoff as distinct threshold
- distance as feasibility
- non-fixed / relational fairness
- private unused object becoming useful elsewhere
- review/reputation as consequential evidence

## Weaker as thesis candidates

### “Second-hand sustainability”
Too generic and not necessarily the user's first motivation.

### “Treasure hunting”
Can be true for some users/categories, but too retail/entertainment-led to define the whole product.

### “Save money”
Important but not authored enough and cannot explain Dolab, proposals, trust or mutual completion.

### “Community”
Potential outcome, but not yet a product truth strong enough to organize the system.

### “Marketplace”
Category label, not meaning.
