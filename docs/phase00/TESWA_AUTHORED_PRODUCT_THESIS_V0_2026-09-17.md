# Teswa — Authored Product Thesis v0

**Date:** 2026-09-17  
**Status:** AUTHORED THESIS — READY FOR DIGITAL TRANSLATION, NOT YET VISUAL SYSTEM

## The thesis

> **Teswa turns what people already own into possibilities between them — carrying each possibility from private possession, through evidence and proposal, into a real exchange that both people confirm.**

Short internal form:

> **PUT WHAT YOU HAVE INTO PLAY.**

This short form is **not final public marketing copy**. It is an internal generative phrase.

---

# 1. Teswa is not a catalog first

A catalog begins with inventory already intended for sale.

Teswa begins earlier.

It begins with:
- something already owned,
- a person deciding it might move,
- uncertainty about what it could become worth to someone else,
- openness to a possibility not fully known yet.

The first product question is therefore not:

> What should I buy?

It can also be:

> What do I already have that could become something else?

This is why Dolab, owner intent, discovery and Offers belong to the same system.

---

# 2. Teswa's central transformation

The defining transformation is:

```text
POSSESSION
→ POSSIBILITY
→ PROPOSAL
→ SHARED COMMITMENT
→ REAL-WORLD OUTCOME
→ EVIDENCE
```

Each arrow changes the meaning of the object.

## POSSESSION

The object belongs to a private world.

It may be:
- used,
- underused,
- loved but replaceable,
- forgotten,
- practical,
- unique,
- ready to leave,
- not yet ready to leave.

## POSSIBILITY

The owner intentionally makes the object available and communicates enough truth + openness for another person to imagine an exchange.

## PROPOSAL

Another person answers the possibility with something of their own.

This is the first explicit commitment threshold.

## SHARED COMMITMENT

Acceptance creates a deal — a new shared object that belongs to neither person's private world alone.

## REAL-WORLD OUTCOME

The exchange still has to survive:
- coordination,
- travel,
- inspection,
- meetup,
- actual handoff.

## EVIDENCE

The completed outcome can create reputation and make future possibilities easier to judge.

---

# 3. The product has three worlds

The future architecture should be understood as movement between **three worlds**, not as a list of screens.

## WORLD A — MINE

Private / owned / preparatory.

Contains:
- my possessions,
- drafts,
- notes,
- media,
- exchange intent,
- things captured from conversation,
- things not yet public.

Current strongest implementation evidence: Dolab.

Product law:

> Nothing in Mine becomes public merely because it exists.

## WORLD B — POSSIBLE

Public / discoverable / interpretable.

Contains:
- available objects,
- people behind them,
- evidence,
- proximity/feasibility,
- owner openness,
- discovery.

This is not necessarily a feed.

It is the field where one person's private object becomes imaginable to another person.

## WORLD C — BETWEEN US

Shared / consequential.

Contains:
- offers,
- offer responses,
- accepted deals,
- coordination,
- contextual evidence,
- mutual completion,
- outcome/review.

This world exists because **two people have now created shared state**.

This is the strongest architecture insight in the thesis:

```text
MINE
↕ intentional publication / withdrawal
POSSIBLE
↕ proposal / response
BETWEEN US
```

The final navigation does not need to use these labels literally.

But the product should preserve their meaning.

---

# 4. Objects in Teswa are not static cards

A Teswa object changes state and social meaning.

The same physical thing can be:

```text
mine privately
→ prepared
→ available
→ proposed against
→ reserved / in a deal
→ swapped
→ part of history
```

Therefore the future item system should treat **state** as part of object identity.

Do not design a universal product card first and bolt state labels onto it later.

The representation should be able to answer, depending on context:
- Is this mine or someone else's?
- Is it private or public?
- Is it available?
- Is someone already acting on it?
- What does the owner want?
- What evidence exists?
- What happened before?
- What can I do next?

---

# 5. Offers are not messages

An Offer is a relationship between two objects and two people.

Current contract:

```text
YOUR ACTIVE ITEM
↔
MY ACTIVE ITEM
```

with:
- sender,
- receiver,
- optional message,
- explicit state,
- consequential response.

Product law:

> **An Offer must remain structurally legible even when conversation surrounds it.**

The proposal should never disappear into an undifferentiated text timeline.

The user must be able to know:
- what was requested,
- what was offered,
- who proposed it,
- what state it is in,
- what changed after each response.

---

# 6. Conversation is context, not one feature

Teswa currently proves three different causes of conversation:

## Person → person

Direct request / access.

## Shared content → conversation

Current example: story reply.

## Accepted exchange → coordination

Deal conversation.

The thesis therefore rejects “Messages” as the conceptual center.

Conversation is infrastructure that appears **inside different relationships**.

Product law:

> **Preserve why people are talking.**

A future unified inbox is possible.
A future generic conversation model is not.

---

# 7. Trust is progressive evidence

Teswa should never pretend trust is one number.

At different moments a user needs different evidence.

Before proposal:
- object media,
- condition,
- owner intent,
- location,
- profile/outcome history.

Before acceptance:
- proposal clarity,
- communication,
- plausibility,
- person evidence.

Before physical handoff:
- meetup feasibility,
- current agreement,
- safety/context,
- item details worth checking.

After handoff:
- mutual completion,
- review.

Thesis law:

> **Show the evidence needed for the next commitment, not every trust signal everywhere.**

This is what “certainty is earned in layers” means operationally.

---

# 8. Teswa crosses the screen boundary

Many digital products can observe completion directly.

Teswa often cannot.

The server can know:
- offer accepted,
- messages sent,
- location shared if supported,
- confirmations submitted.

It cannot inherently know:
- whether the people met,
- whether the object matched expectations,
- whether the actual handoff happened.

Therefore:

> **The app must be exact about the difference between digital state and physical reality.**

Accepted is not completed.
Scheduled is not happened.
One person's confirmation is not mutual completion.

This distinction should become a core interaction quality later.

---

# 9. Dolab is the private half of the product

Dolab should no longer be treated as a feature competing for a tab.

The more useful question is:

> What private capabilities must exist before something is ready to become a public possibility?

Current Dolab evidence already includes:
- private items,
- draft/ready state,
- media,
- notes,
- exchange intent,
- saved conversation material,
- publication bridge,
- published-item linkage.

Therefore the thesis assigns Dolab a role even if the final UI renames/restructures it:

> **Teswa needs a private object world where ownership can be prepared before it becomes social.**

The name “Dolab” may survive, evolve or become a deeper system metaphor later.

The role survives.

---

# 10. Discovery is possibility matching

Teswa should not begin from the assumption that discovery is a product grid.

The meaningful question is:

> Which available possibilities are worth putting in front of this person now?

Signals may include:
- expressed desire,
- own possessions / potential offer inventory,
- location / effort,
- category,
- people / social context,
- novelty,
- explicit search,
- prior behavior.

Current `DesireMode` is especially valuable because it proves users can express different kinds of openness:
- specific,
- flexible,
- surprise.

That can eventually support differentiated discovery behavior rather than one ranking logic for everyone.

No algorithm is specified here.

# 11. What Teswa should feel like — before styling

This section defines interaction character, not visuals.

## PERSONAL, NOT MERCHANT-LIKE

The objects come from people's lives, not warehouse stock.

## CONSEQUENTIAL, NOT HEAVY

Offers/deals matter, but the product should not make every interaction bureaucratic.

## ALIVE, NOT CHAOTIC

Possibility/discovery can feel dynamic; commitment states must become exact.

## HUMAN, NOT VAGUE

Soft rejection and “thinking” are socially nuanced, but state should still be clear.

## TRUSTWORTHY, NOT AUTHORITARIAN

Teswa should expose useful evidence and let people judge; it should not invent certainty or score “fairness” it cannot know.

## PHYSICAL, NOT WEB-LIKE

The product crosses into real places, objects, voice, photos, movement and handoff. Native Android behavior should eventually exploit this reality instead of rendering a website inside a phone-shaped frame.

# 12. Product laws derived from the thesis

These are now strong enough to constrain future work.

### LAW 1
**Private is a real state.**
Do not force every possession directly into public listing form.

### LAW 2
**Publication is a threshold.**
Crossing Mine → Possible must feel intentional and reversible where the contract allows it.

### LAW 3
**Availability is not enough.**
Owner openness / desire is part of the possibility.

### LAW 4
**Proposal is structured.**
Never reduce an Offer to chat text.

### LAW 5
**Acceptance creates shared state.**
A Deal is a new relationship object, not a success toast.

### LAW 6
**Context travels with commitment.**
Item / offer / person / deal context should not disappear when conversation opens.

### LAW 7
**Trust evidence is progressive.**
Show relevant evidence near the decision it supports.

### LAW 8
**Digital agreement is not physical completion.**
Never visually collapse accepted / coordinating / mutually completed states.

### LAW 9
**History matters after action.**
Offers, deals and reviews create durable object/person history.

### LAW 10
**Social features must strengthen possibility, trust or exchange.**
Otherwise they are engagement residue and should be removed or demoted.

# 13. Features that now have to re-earn themselves against the thesis

## STORIES

Survive only if they create:
- meaningful discovery,
- object/person context,
- trust,
- or useful conversation openings.

“Stories increase engagement” is not enough.

## FOLLOW / PEOPLE

Survive only if relationships improve:
- discovery relevance,
- trust,
- repeated exchange,
- or access to meaningful inventory.

## MOTION / CITY PULSE

Survive only if they make real local possibility more legible or actionable.

Novel motion is not justification.

## NOTIFICATIONS

Must map to meaningful state change:
- new possibility,
- proposal response,
- commitment action,
- coordination,
- trust/outcome.

Notification volume is not success.

---

# 14. What this thesis explicitly rejects

Teswa is not authored as:
- “Egyptian Vinted”,
- “Facebook Marketplace but barter”,
- “OLX with social features”,
- “a sustainability marketplace”,
- “a social network for second-hand goods”,
- “a treasure-hunting app”,
- “a community-first commerce platform”.

Those descriptions either copy a category or overfit one supporting behavior.

Teswa's authored center is the transformation itself:

> **mine → possible → proposed → shared → real → remembered**

# 15. Authored thesis — canonical internal version

Long form:

> **Teswa is a native exchange system that turns possessions already living in one person's private world into possibilities with another person. It preserves the object's truth, the owner's openness, the proposal that joins two possessions, the evidence needed to trust the next step, and the difference between digital agreement and a real exchange that both people confirm.**

Compressed internal phrase:

> **PUT WHAT YOU HAVE INTO PLAY.**

State spine:

> **MINE → POSSIBLE → BETWEEN US → REAL → EVIDENCE**

These three lines now form the authored product thesis for the next gate.
