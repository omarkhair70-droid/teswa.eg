# TESWA VISUAL CONSTITUTION V2

**Date:** 2026-09-17  
**Status:** ART-DIRECTION AUTHORITY — DRAFT 01 / ANCHOR-SCREEN PHASE  
**Applies to:** `android-native/`  
**Relationship to V1:** V1 remains the product/system architecture authority. This document defines the authored visual identity that V1 did not yet fully express.

---

## 0. Why this document exists

The native rebuild solved a real problem: Teswa finally understands what it is.

The current product structure is clearer than the old Expo product:

`MINE → POSSIBLE → BETWEEN US → REAL → EVIDENCE`

But the current native presentation stopped too early. It has a coherent palette, typography, spacing, component family and semantic hierarchy, yet much of the shipped composition still reads as a clean Material implementation rather than an authored Teswa world.

The old Expo product had the opposite problem. It often had more visual personality, authored moments, gradients, overlaps, media treatment and small signs of life, but the product meaning was less disciplined. Marketplace, stories, social presence, dashboards and experimentation competed for attention.

We are not choosing old vs new.

We are combining:

- **the old product's willingness to author a world**;
- **the native rebuild's product clarity and state truth**;
- **a new visual grammar that makes ordinary possessions feel lived-in, transferable and memorable**.

The result must communicate two things immediately:

> **"This is an app for exchanging things between people."**

and, at the same time:

> **"This place has a point of view. It was made by someone, not assembled from a UI kit."**

# 1. TWO LAYERS, NOT ONE

## 1.1 Product layer — EXCHANGE FIELD

`EXCHANGE FIELD` remains the structural system.

It defines:

- private vs public;
- one possession becoming a possibility;
- one item ↔ one item;
- offer as commitment threshold;
- accepted offer becoming a Deal;
- reality requiring bilateral completion;
- completed exchange becoming evidence.

It makes Teswa understandable.

## 1.2 Art-direction layer — OBJECTS WITH A PAST — OBJECTS WITH A NEXT

This is the visual/emotional law.

> **OBJECTS WITH A PAST — OBJECTS WITH A NEXT.**

Internal Arabic interpretation:

> **الحاجة جاية من حياة، ورايحة لاحتمال جديد.**

This is not user-facing marketing copy that must appear everywhere. It is the rule used to design the product.

The item is not a SKU.

It has already lived somewhere. It may contain wear, memory, context, taste, ownership and a small story. Teswa then gives it a possible next life with somebody else.

The interface therefore does not merely display inventory. It visually carries an object through a lifecycle.

# 2. DESIGN ARCHAEOLOGY — WHAT THE OLD EXPO PRODUCT TAUGHT US

The Expo product is not a visual source of truth and must not be copied screen-for-screen. It is a **design archaeology archive**: months of experiments that reveal where Teswa already showed signs of personality.

## 2.1 KEEP THE INSTINCT

Useful instincts found in the old product include:

### Authored hero moments
`HomeLivingWorldHero` did not behave like a stock toolbar. It created a composed moment using gradient atmosphere, soft orbs, a small brand signal, asymmetrical actions and a live-state cue.

**Lesson:** Teswa needs authored moments at important product thresholds.

### Layering and overlap
`ProfileLivingHero` used cover, avatar overlap, glow layers and an identity panel rather than laying every piece of information in one flat column.

**Lesson:** meaningful overlap can create depth and identity when it represents a relationship between layers.

### Media given room
The old product often let photography dominate before metadata.

**Lesson:** the object should usually be experienced before it is classified.

### Small living signals
Subtle dots, marks, short labels and micro-states made the old product feel alive.

**Lesson:** Teswa can have tiny authored signals without turning into a dashboard.

### A willingness to be custom
The old app did not always look like the default component library.

**Lesson:** we should preserve that confidence while removing the old inconsistency.

## 2.2 DO NOT BRING BACK THE CONFUSION

We explicitly do **not** revive:

- dashboard thinking on the Discover home;
- social vanity metrics as product purpose;
- decorative gradients on every card;
- every metadata value becoming a pill;
- card-inside-card-inside-card structures;
- stories as a parallel social universe;
- likes/followers dominating object possibility;
- equal visual weight for every feature;
- different art direction per screen;
- arbitrary icon mixtures;
- fake nostalgia applied as a skin.

The old Expo `ItemCard`, for example, had more styling than the current native rows, but still treated the item as a marketplace card with multiple metadata pills, like state and video badges, owner row and decorative gradient container. That is not the final direction.

We keep its confidence in media and authored detail, not its marketplace-card grammar.

# 3. EXTERNAL REFERENCE EXTRACTION

References are mechanics, not visual clones.

## 3.1 Are.na — meaning accumulates through context

Are.na's core idea is that a block can exist inside many channels and accumulates context over time. The same block means something different depending on the collection around it.

### Teswa translation

The same object must remain visually identifiable while its context changes:

`private possession → public possibility → requested/offered object → deal object → completed evidence`

We should not redraw it as a generic new card at every step.

The object is one durable thing whose meaning accumulates.

**Take:** continuity, context accumulation, calm collection logic.  
**Do not take:** neutral research-tool aesthetics as the visual identity.

Reference:
- https://www.are.na/about
- https://help.are.na/docs/getting-started/blocks

## 3.2 Pinterest Shuffles — objects can become compositional material

Shuffles treats images as cuttable objects that can be layered, rotated, arranged and animated rather than forcing every image into a rectangular feed card.

### Teswa translation

Use object cutout/overlap logic selectively in authored moments:

- discovery clusters;
- publication transition;
- offer pair;
- share card;
- empty/onboarding art;
- completed-exchange evidence.

Do not turn the entire app into a collage editor.

**Take:** layering, object extraction, tactile composition, animation around objects.  
**Do not take:** maximal collage density or playful chaos in commitment flows.

References:
- https://newsroom.pinterest.com/news/shuffles-by-pinterest-expands-to-nine-additional-countries/
- https://help.pinterest.com/en/article/shuffles

## 3.3 Poolsuite — nostalgia works only when it is a language

Poolsuite is effective because nostalgia is not one filter. Interface chrome, typography, tone, interaction and imagery all belong to the same remembered world.

### Teswa translation

If nostalgia enters Teswa, it must be coherent and specific to **lived objects**, not a random retro theme.

Teswa is **not** a fake Windows/Mac interface.

Our nostalgia comes from:

- personal photographs;
- old labels;
- date/location marks;
- paper edges;
- handwriting traces;
- storage and archive behavior;
- signs of use and transfer.

**Take:** commitment to one remembered material world.  
**Do not take:** retro operating-system chrome.

Reference:
- Apple App Store editorial on Poolsuite FM: old-school interface and mid-90s visual identity.

## 3.4 MUBI / Notebook — editorial identity can coexist with a functioning product

MUBI and Notebook show that typography, photography, pacing and editorial composition can make a product feel authored without making ordinary interaction illegible.

### Teswa translation

Discovery can have editorial pacing while offer/deal controls remain direct and native.

We separate:

- **remembered surfaces** — authored;
- **routine controls** — quiet and native.

Reference:
- D&AD Notebook archive, 2023.

## 3.5 Museum of Broken Relationships — ordinary object + short story = human connection

The useful mechanic is not heartbreak. It is the observation that an ordinary possession becomes emotionally legible when paired with a short, specific human context.

### Teswa translation

Item story/context is optional and subordinate, but when it exists it should feel like a trace of ownership rather than another database field.

An object does not need a dramatic story to have a past.

**Take:** human trace, compact captioning, respect for ordinary possessions.  
**Do not take:** museum solemnity or forced sentimentality.

# 4. THE CORE VISUAL LAW

## 4.1 The object comes first

Before title, category, metrics and buttons, the user should feel the object.

Photography may be:

- full-bleed;
- unusually cropped;
- offset from the grid;
- layered over paper/field;
- shown as a cutout in rare authored contexts;
- paired with a small physical-feeling annotation.

Metadata must not bury the object.

## 4.2 Every object has two visual dimensions

### PRESENT
What is it now?

- image;
- title;
- condition;
- current owner;
- current state.

### TRACE
What tells us it existed before this moment?

- optional story;
- date/location context;
- private note while in Dolab;
- condition wear;
- prior state;
- completed exchange mark later.

Trace is never fabricated. If no history exists, the UI stays quiet.

## 4.3 The product visually changes when the relationship changes

The UI should not look identical before and after commitment.

- Discover is open and asymmetric.
- Dolab is private and contained.
- Offer is relational and paired.
- Deal is bound and operational.
- Completed is archival and evidentiary.

This state-dependent visual architecture is mandatory.

# 5. NOSTALGIA WITHOUT COSTUME

Teswa nostalgia is **domestic / personal / object memory**, not generic retro.

## 5.1 Allowed materials

Use selectively:

- warm uncoated-paper fields;
- photo-print borders;
- slightly imperfect cropping;
- restrained paper labels;
- small date/location stamps;
- catalogue/archive numbering;
- handwritten **marks**, not paragraphs;
- vector underlines/circles/arrows that feel manually placed;
- tape/clip/seam motifs in rare hero moments;
- subtle print/scan texture at low opacity;
- off-register or imperfect edge treatment only where it remains readable;
- tiny provenance captions.

## 5.2 Forbidden nostalgia clichés

Do not use:

- sepia over everything;
- film grain over every screen;
- fake polaroid frames around every image;
- fake torn paper everywhere;
- typewriter font for body copy;
- handwritten font for dynamic Arabic UI;
- VHS noise as decoration;
- random stickers;
- retro desktop windows;
- skeuomorphic drawers that make navigation slower.

Nostalgia must come from **meaning + material**, not props.

# 6. CUSTOM VS SYSTEM ICONS

Teswa currently uses a unified Material Rounded icon family. Keep that for utility.

## 6.1 Utility stays system-native

Keep Material/system icons for:

- back;
- close;
- settings;
- camera;
- gallery;
- microphone;
- delete;
- archive;
- report;
- share;
- notifications;
- search;
- refresh;
- send;
- generic location.

These should not become decorative illustration projects.

## 6.2 Teswa-owned concepts get custom marks

Create a small SVG/Compose-vector family for concepts that define Teswa itself.

### MINE / DOLAB MARK

**Concept:** a wardrobe/cabinet reduced to a compact symbol.

Not a literal furniture illustration.

Possible anatomy:

- outer frame;
- central seam / two doors;
- one tiny shelf or handle cue;
- slightly asymmetric internal division.

At 24dp it reads as a distinctive inventory/wardrobe mark. At larger sizes its geometry may expand into shelf/divider layout motifs.

### POSSIBLE / DISCOVER MARK

**Concept:** an opening / aperture / reveal.

A shape where part of an object appears through a boundary.

Meaning: something that was private is now visible as possibility.

Avoid compass cliché as the authored root mark.

### BETWEEN US MARK

**Concept:** two independent forms linked by one relation.

It should not simply be `SwapHoriz`.

Candidate grammar:

- two object nodes;
- one soft bridge/seam/thread between them;
- relation visible only when both sides exist.

This geometry becomes the foundation for Offer/Deal compositions.

### ME / EVIDENCE MARK

**Concept:** identity as archive, not generic account.

Candidate grammar:

- portrait/identity frame;
- small archival notch/stamp/evidence mark;
- one accumulated trace.

Utility profile screens may still use `Person` where required; the root identity mark is authored.

### PUT INTO PLAY MARK

**Concept:** an object crossing a boundary.

This is the authored visual verb of Teswa.

A compact object/shape begins inside a private frame and crosses into an open field.

It may become:

- publish icon;
- transition animation;
- loading/confirmation moment;
- onboarding explanation;
- brand motion asset.

# 7. OBJECT LIFECYCLE AS A VISUAL TRANSFORMATION

One object's visual identity persists while its state treatment evolves.

## 7.1 PRIVATE / DRAFT

Feeling: kept, personal, unfinished.

Treatment:

- softer contrast;
- image may sit like a raw snapshot;
- private annotation/notes can appear nearby;
- no public-performance chrome;
- private boundary visible.

## 7.2 READY

Feeling: prepared, still mine.

Treatment:

- object becomes cleaner/more deliberate;
- readiness mark appears;
- public fields can be previewed;
- still visibly inside Dolab.

## 7.3 IN PLAY / PUBLIC

Feeling: open possibility.

Treatment:

- image gains space;
- boundary opens;
- metadata is reduced;
- clay authored action enters;
- public owner/context becomes legible.

## 7.4 OFFER

Feeling: two separate possessions touching for the first time.

Treatment:

- one persistent `ExchangePair` composition;
- no cart/checkout metaphor;
- two objects keep distinct identity;
- relation mark joins them;
- optional message remains secondary.

## 7.5 DEAL

Feeling: relation became durable.

Treatment:

- pair compresses into a stable bound header;
- bridge/seam becomes stronger;
- coordination exists below the pair;
- object identity never disappears behind chat.

## 7.6 COMPLETED

Feeling: physical reality became evidence.

Treatment:

- calm archival settle;
- date/outcome trace;
- completed mark/stamp;
- object remains historically accessible;
- no confetti.

## 7.7 ARCHIVED

Feeling: tucked away, not erased.

Treatment:

- lower chroma / quieter field;
- visually stored, not deleted;
- history remains legible.

# 8. COMPOSITION GRAMMAR

The current native UI overuses a repeated pattern:

`screen heading → explanation → chip/row → row → row`

V2 must introduce authored composition without losing structure.

## 8.1 Rule: asymmetry belongs to media, not controls

Buttons, inputs and important actions stay aligned and predictable.

Photography, object stages, annotations and editorial clusters may break symmetry.

## 8.2 Rule: one memorable move per viewport

A screen should usually contain one authored visual event, not five.

Examples:

- one object escaping the grid;
- one overlap;
- one relationship line;
- one archival stamp;
- one large image crop.

Everything else supports it.

## 8.3 Rule: rhythm alternates

Discovery must not repeat identical blocks forever.

Use a controlled sequence such as:

1. hero object moment;
2. compact two-object cluster;
3. story/context interruption;
4. full-width object;
5. nearby practical block;
6. object pair/curated cluster.

The data remains dynamic; the composition system has a finite set of authored templates.

## 8.4 Rule: no decorative container without a reason

V1's no-card-soup rule remains.

The solution to generic UI is **not** more cards.

---

# 9. IMAGE GRAMMAR

## 9.1 Photography is not normalized into one thumbnail ratio

Support a bounded set of image shapes:

- portrait object stage;
- 4:3 domestic snapshot;
- square compact object;
- wide contextual image;
- object cutout in rare authored slots.

## 9.2 The image determines the crop, not the template alone

Where technically feasible, focal information should survive cropping.

## 9.3 Missing image is not a gray rectangle

A missing-image state should still belong to Teswa:

- custom object-placeholder mark;
- quiet archive label;
- category/title carries more weight;
- no giant generic image icon.

## 9.4 User photography must remain honest

Do not auto-filter user images into fake film photography.

Optional presentation texture may exist around the image, not destructively over the source.

---

# 10. TYPOGRAPHY GRAMMAR

Arabic remains primary.

We do not need an ornamental Arabic UI font to have personality.

Personality comes from:

- scale contrast;
- line breaks;
- placement;
- relationship with images;
- tiny archival labels;
- sparse Latin/number notation where meaningful.

## 10.1 Product copy

Warm, calm Egyptian Arabic.

## 10.2 Archival micro-labels

Very short labels may feel more catalog-like:

- date;
- area;
- object number;
- state;
- transfer/completion marker.

They must remain readable and not mimic bureaucracy.

## 10.3 Handwritten layer

Dynamic copy never uses a handwriting font.

Handwritten feeling is reserved for authored SVG marks:

- underline;
- circle;
- tiny arrow;
- one short fixed mark if it becomes part of the brand asset family.

# 11. ROOT WORLD SIGNATURES

Each root belongs to the same Teswa universe but has a distinct spatial character.

## 11.1 POSSIBLE / اكتشف — THE OPEN FIELD

**Feeling:** accidental discovery, air, possibility.

### Signature

- open composition;
- media leads;
- variable vertical rhythm;
- objects can break the strict column locally;
- occasional human context;
- nearby is practical and quiet;
- stories are contextual interruptions, not a social rail copied from Instagram.

### Screen-entry moment

One strong object/pair appears quickly. Search and notifications remain accessible but do not dominate the top 30% of the screen.

### Kill

- title + paragraph consuming the whole first viewport;
- horizontal chip bars as the main personality;
- identical ObjectStage repeated indefinitely;
- marketplace grid;
- dashboard summary.

---

## 11.2 MINE / دولابي — THE PRIVATE CABINET

**Feeling:** personal storage, preparation, ownership, memory.

### Signature

The wardrobe/cabinet mark grows into the layout.

Possible spatial mechanics:

- faint shelf/divider geometry;
- objects grouped by lifecycle state rather than generic filter-only rows;
- drafts may sit like snapshots/notes;
- ready objects feel prepared but still inside the private frame;
- published objects visibly cross or sit beyond a boundary;
- archive feels tucked away rather than deleted.

### The key moment

`حط حاجة` starts with the object/image, not form fields.

### Kill

- large explainer card permanently occupying the top;
- every object as same row;
- filter chips carrying the entire state model;
- public marketplace visual language inside private Dolab.

---

## 11.3 BETWEEN US / بيننا — THE RELATION FIELD

**Feeling:** two sides, one changing relationship.

This should become the most recognizably Teswa screen family.

### Signature

Every meaningful row/card visually answers:

- what is mine/theirs?
- who is on the other side?
- what is the relationship state now?
- what needs to happen next?

For offers/deals, show the two objects visually whenever useful.

A relationship line/seam/bridge links two identities.

### Hub

Do not render accepted/pending exchange as generic message rows with an icon square.

A compact activity row may use:

`small object A  — relation mark — small object B`

with person/state/timestamp secondary.

Direct/contextual conversation rows can remain person-led because there may be no object pair.

### Deal

The pair becomes a persistent bound header. Conversation exists **under** the relationship, not instead of it.

### Kill

- inbox-first visual identity;
- same row component for offer, deal, direct and story reply;
- state pill doing all the communication;
- hiding objects after acceptance.

---

## 11.4 ME / أنا — THE LIVING ARCHIVE

**Feeling:** identity built from real outcomes, not social performance.

### Signature

Profile is a personal archive composition:

- portrait/identity;
- a few lived traces;
- successful exchanges as evidence;
- reviews/history as pages in the archive;
- no generic dashboard stats strip.

Cover image is not allowed to become a meaningless giant black/empty rectangle. If no authored cover exists, use a purposeful identity composition rather than a blank fallback.

### Kill

- giant empty cover placeholder;
- follower/following as primary identity;
- evidence as two generic rows only;
- item-management controls living permanently in profile.

# 13. INITIAL AUTHORED PRIMITIVE FAMILY

V2 should add a **small** family of high-value primitives rather than replace every Material component.

Candidate components:

1. `TeswaWorldMark` — custom marks for Possible/Mine/BetweenUs/Me.
2. `TeswaObjectMoment` — media-led authored object presentation with bounded layout variants.
3. `TeswaObjectTrace` — optional date/place/story/private-note annotation treatment.
4. `TeswaCabinetField` — Dolab lifecycle grouping / shelf geometry.
5. `TeswaRelationPair` — richer visual descendant of ExchangePair.
6. `TeswaRelationThread` — bridge/seam/line between two durable entities.
7. `TeswaArchiveStamp` — completed/exchanged evidence mark.
8. `TeswaObjectPlaceholder` — authored missing-image identity.
9. `TeswaThresholdMotion` — reusable object-crossing-boundary transition for publish/accept/complete.

Routine form controls remain Material-based.

# 14. MOTION IDENTITY

V1 defined motion timing. V2 defines what motion **means visually**.

## 14.1 Possession → possibility

The object crosses a boundary.

Private field recedes; object image gains space; public state appears.

## 14.2 Possibility → offer

Requested object remains anchored while owned object arrives from the user's side to form a pair.

## 14.3 Offer → deal

The two objects do not disappear.

The pair settles/compresses; the relation line becomes durable; coordination appears below it.

## 14.4 Deal → completed evidence

The relationship settles into an archival outcome mark. No celebration theater.

## 14.5 Reduced motion

Every transition must collapse into a clear static state when animation is reduced/disabled.

# 15. COLOR / MATERIAL DIRECTION

The current Clay / Sage / Paper system remains a useful semantic foundation, but V2 treats it as **ink/material roles**, not the entire identity.

### Paper
Warm lived surface, not sterile white.

### Ink
Near-black/brown for human, printed warmth.

### Clay
Action / authored exchange energy.

### Sage
Trust / calm evidence / resolved relationship.

### Amber
Attention / unresolved significance.

### Photo black
Deep near-black permitted around immersive media, never as an accidental empty-cover fallback.

### Archive neutrals
A restrained family of paper, label, graphite and faded-print neutrals may be added during anchor implementation.

Rule:

> **Texture and material cannot compensate for weak composition.**

Do not tune ten new colors before the anchor compositions work in grayscale.

# 16. PORTFOLIO-LEVEL QUALITY BAR

Teswa should be able to survive this test:

Show an experienced designer one screen with the logo hidden.

They should still be able to believe:

> "This belongs to the same product as the other Teswa screens."

Then show the root worlds side by side.

They should feel related but not duplicated.

Then show the object lifecycle from Dolab → Discover → Offer → Deal → Evidence.

The same possession should feel like one thing traveling through states, not five unrelated cards.

Finally, show the product next to standard Material marketplace apps.

Teswa should remain usable and Android-native while clearly carrying an authored identity.
